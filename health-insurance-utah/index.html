Why a national PPO network matters in Utah

Founders and remote workers travel often, and southern Utah residents spend time in Nevada and Arizona. We confirm your doctors and hospitals, including Intermountain Health and University of Utah Health, before you apply.

Private PPO vs. HealthCare.gov

Utah uses HealthCare.gov. Open Enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 to January 15, 2027; choose a plan by December 15 for a January 1 start. The Marketplace makes sense for some people, but healthy Utah professionals above the 2027 subsidy limit pay full price there, often for a narrower HMO or EPO network, and anyone who underestimates income now repays the full excess credit. Many private plans also accept applications year-round instead of only during Open Enrollment.

Private plans aren't guaranteed: they ask health questions first. A pre-existing condition can be excluded or lead to a decline. If that's a concern for anyone in your family, we'll compare a HealthCare.gov plan alongside it and say plainly if it's the better fit.

Self-employed in Utah? Your premiums are usually deductible

If you're self-employed with a net profit, premiums for you, your spouse and dependents are generally deductible on IRS Form 7206. Months when you or a spouse could join an employer plan with an employer contribution don't count. Confirm the details with your CPA.

Earn too much for a subsidy in Utah?

If your household earns more than 400% of the federal poverty level, you won't get a premium tax credit in Utah for 2027. That's $63,840 for one person, $86,560 for two and $132,000 for a family of four. Above that line, every Marketplace plan is full price.

Picture a single 1099 consultant in Lehi–Provo making $72,000. That's above the $63,840 limit for one person, so there's no credit on HealthCare.gov. Private, medically underwritten PPO plans use age, location and health rather than income, so for healthy households above the cliff they're often the stronger value, with national networks.

This applies to self-employed business owners, 1099 contractors, commission earners and salaried professionals without an employer plan. Self-employed people with a net profit can usually deduct premiums on IRS Form 7206, private plans included. See what to do if you earn too much for a subsidy and our 2027 subsidy calculator.

Offered COBRA after leaving a Utah employer? Before you elect, compare. COBRA means the full premium plus up to 2% for up to 18 months; a private PPO for a healthy applicant is often cheaper and works across the U.S. Our COBRA alternatives in Utah guide explains the timing.

Paying too much for insurance through your job? Healthy Utah households above the subsidy line often find a private PPO cheaper than adding family to an employer plan. We'll compare your paycheck cost side by side and plan the timing around your employer's open enrollment.

Between jobs or retiring before 65 in Utah? A gap in coverage is when overpaying is easiest. COBRA charges the full premium, and for healthy people a private PPO usually costs less, whether you need a few months until a new job's plan starts or several years until Medicare. See health insurance between jobs and early retirement health insurance.

Who a private PPO fits best in Utah: healthy adults and families who travel, want a national network and earn above the subsidy line. If someone in your household has a serious ongoing condition, a Marketplace plan can still be the better choice because it can't turn you down, and we'll show you both side by side.

Is Private Health Insurance Worth It in Florida?

For healthy people who pay full price, often yes. For people with health conditions or a meaningful tax credit, often no. Here's how to tell which side you're on.

Reviewed by Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749) · November 11, 2026

Get a Free Quote Call Us Now
Key points
  • Private plans are usually worth comparing when you're healthy and don't qualify for a tax credit.
  • They are usually not the right fit with a serious health condition, a planned pregnancy or a meaningful tax credit.
  • The main gains are PPO freedom, health-based pricing and year-round applications.
  • The main costs are underwriting, possible exclusions and benefits that may not match ACA plans.
Ayoub’s take

If you've been treated for anything in the past few years, tell me up front. It changes which plans are worth applying for, and it can save you a declined application.

Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749)

Private health insurance is worth it when its health-based price buys you a better network and protection than the plan you'd otherwise pay full price for. That's most often true for healthy individuals and families above the subsidy limit. It's rarely true for people who need the protections only ACA plans guarantee.

Who it's usually worth it for

  • Healthy adults and families who don't qualify for a premium tax credit.
  • Business owners, professionals and executives who want a PPO and the freedom to choose specialists.
  • People with a second home, or who travel, who want a plan built on a national network.
  • Anyone who misses Open Enrollment and is healthy enough to apply for a private plan.

Who it's usually not right for

  • Anyone with a serious or ongoing health condition, which private plans can limit or exclude.
  • Couples planning a pregnancy, since maternity can be limited on private plans.
  • Households that qualify for a meaningful tax credit, which only Marketplace plans receive.

What you gain and what you give up

You may gainYou may give up
A premium priced on your health, which can be lower if you're healthyGuaranteed acceptance; you answer health questions and can be declined
PPO access to specialists without referralsCoverage for some pre-existing conditions
National networks on some plansEvery essential health benefit, such as maternity, on some plans
Applying any time of yearThe ACA's standard plan rules; each plan's own terms apply

How higher-earning families often put it together

Many of our clients combine a private or individual PPO for broad access with an HSA-eligible plan design when it fits, so they can set aside pre-tax money for care. Some also pay separately for a concierge or direct primary care practice for easier access to a primary doctor. A concierge membership isn't insurance and doesn't replace a major medical plan; it sits alongside one.

Five questions to ask before you apply

  1. How does the plan treat any condition I've had or am being treated for?
  2. Does it cover maternity, mental health and prescriptions, and how?
  3. Is the network national or regional, and are my doctors in it?
  4. What are the deductible and out-of-pocket maximum, and what counts toward them?
  5. Is the plan renewable, and can the price change based on my claims?

FAQ

Private health insurance questions

What are the downsides of private health insurance?

You answer health questions and can be declined, pre-existing conditions can be limited or excluded, and some plans don't cover every essential health benefit, such as maternity.

Can I be denied private health insurance?

Yes. Private, medically underwritten plans can decline an application or offer coverage with limits based on your health history. ACA Marketplace plans can't.

Is private health insurance cheaper than the Marketplace?

For healthy people who pay full Marketplace price, it often can be. For people with tax credits or health conditions, the Marketplace is usually cheaper.

Does private health insurance cover pre-existing conditions?

It depends on the plan and the condition. Some private plans limit or exclude pre-existing conditions. We review each plan's terms with you before you apply.

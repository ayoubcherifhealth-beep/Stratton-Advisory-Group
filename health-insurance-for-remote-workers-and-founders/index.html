Health Insurance for Remote Workers & Founders

Health insurance for startup founders and remote workers: private PPO plans for you and your family, then group or ICHRA benefits when you hire.

Reviewed by Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749) · Updated November 17, 2026

Get a Free Quote Call Us Now

Founders often cover themselves before raising money or hiring, and remote employees sometimes get an employer plan built for another state. A private PPO with a national network works wherever you live and travel, and founders can move to a group plan or ICHRA as the team grows.

Founders and remote workers have opposite problems

Founders usually have no plan at all until the company can offer one, and then have to choose between a small group plan, an ICHRA and individual coverage. Remote workers usually have a plan, but one built around the employer's home state, which can mean few doctors where they actually live. Both problems come back to the same check: does the network cover the ZIP code where you live and the places you travel?

Healthy founders often cover their own families with a private PPO before raising money, and S corporation owners handle premiums through payroll. Remote employees with a thin network can compare a private PPO with the employer plan.

What matters for remote workers and founders

  • Before the first hire: a private PPO for you and your family.
  • First employees: compare a small group plan, ICHRA or QSEHRA.
  • Remote employer plan: check whether its network actually includes doctors where you live.

Between jobs or retiring early?

A gap in coverage is where people overpay most, usually on COBRA. For healthy people a private PPO usually costs less and can start any time. See health insurance between jobs.

The honest caveat: private PPO plans are medically underwritten. They can limit or exclude pre-existing conditions and approval isn't guaranteed. If someone in your household has a serious ongoing condition, a Marketplace plan can be the better choice because it can't turn you down, and we'll tell you if that's you.

Alabama: Huntsville's engineers and Birmingham's startups

Huntsville's aerospace and defense economy has produced engineering spin-offs and consultants, and Birmingham has a growing startup scene around Innovation Depot. Many remote workers in Alabama work for companies based in Atlanta, Texas or the Northeast.

Solo founders usually start with their own private PPO; once you hire in Huntsville or Birmingham, compare a small group plan with an ICHRA. Engineers leaving defense contractors often have generous employer plans to replace, so compare deductibles carefully.

Arkansas: Northwest Arkansas startups and corporate spin-offs

Northwest Arkansas has a growing startup and supplier ecosystem around large corporate headquarters, and Little Rock has its own tech community. Remote workers have moved to Bentonville and Fayetteville for trails and lower costs.

Supplier and logistics startups in Northwest Arkansas often hire quickly. Decide early whether you'll offer a group plan or an ICHRA, because changing later is harder than starting right.

Colorado: Denver and Boulder startups

Denver and Boulder have long been startup hubs, and Colorado draws remote workers who chose the mountains over an office. Colorado runs its own marketplace, Connect for Health Colorado.

Denver and Boulder founders often hire remote employees in other states. With an ICHRA, every employee picks an individual-market plan in their home state; a Colorado small group plan may leave out-of-state hires with network gaps.

Delaware: Delaware-incorporated startups and real Delaware founders

Many startups are incorporated in Delaware without operating there, but Wilmington has its own founders and remote workers commuting to Philadelphia offices a few days a month. Your health plan follows where you live, not where the company is incorporated.

Founders actually based in Wilmington often hire in Philadelphia and New Jersey too. Check how any group plan's network handles employees who live across state lines.

Atlanta has a large startup community around Midtown and Tech Square near Georgia Tech, along with fintech and logistics companies, and many remote workers live in the suburbs. Georgia runs its own marketplace, Georgia Access.

Chicago startups often hire employees who live in Indiana or Wisconsin. An ICHRA handles that by reimbursing each person for a plan in their own state, whereas a group plan needs a network that covers all three.

FAQ

Remote Workers & Founders: questions

How do startup founders get health insurance?

Usually with a private PPO before hiring, then a small group plan, ICHRA or QSEHRA once there are employees.

What if my remote job's health plan has no doctors near me?

Check the network first. If it doesn't work where you live, a private PPO for family members may be a better fit.

My startup is shutting down. Can I keep the group plan?

Only while the plan exists. Federal COBRA and state continuation end if the company cancels the plan, so apply for new coverage right away.

Why does my employer plan have so few doctors where I live?

Many employer plans are built around the company's home state. Check the network for your ZIP code; if it's thin, compare a private PPO with a national network.

Health Insurance for Realtors

Health insurance for real estate agents on commission: private PPO plans, deductible premiums and coverage that doesn't depend on a good or bad year.

Reviewed by Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749) · Updated November 17, 2026

Get a Free Quote Call Us Now

Most agents are independent contractors with income that swings year to year, which makes income-based Marketplace pricing risky: underestimate and you may repay the whole credit. A private PPO is priced on health, not income, so healthy agents above the subsidy line usually get more predictable value.

Commission income and income-based pricing don't mix

Most agents are independent contractors, so no brokerage is choosing a plan for you. Marketplace subsidies are based on a guess of your income for the year, and starting with tax year 2026, excess advance credits must be repaid in full, which is a real risk in a year that turns out better than expected. A private PPO is priced on age, ZIP code and health, so your premium doesn't change with your closings.

Premiums are generally deductible on IRS Form 7206 in months your business has a net profit. In the states below with no state income tax on wages, that deduction only lowers your federal tax.

What matters for realtors

  • Commission swings: private PPO pricing doesn't change with your income.
  • Deduction: agents with a net profit can generally deduct premiums on Form 7206.
  • Brokerage offers: discount cards and fixed-indemnity plans aren't full comprehensive coverage.

Between jobs or retiring early?

A gap in coverage is where people overpay most, usually on COBRA. For healthy people a private PPO usually costs less and can start any time. See health insurance between jobs.

The honest caveat: private PPO plans are medically underwritten. They can limit or exclude pre-existing conditions and approval isn't guaranteed. If someone in your household has a serious ongoing condition, a Marketplace plan can be the better choice because it can't turn you down, and we'll tell you if that's you.

Alabama: Birmingham suburbs, Huntsville growth and the Gulf beaches

Huntsville has been one of the fastest-growing cities in the South, Birmingham's suburbs in Shelby County keep agents busy and Baldwin County's beaches, from Gulf Shores to Orange Beach, add second-home and vacation-rental sales. Commission income in a beach market can be very seasonal.

Beach agents in Baldwin County earn most of their commission in spring and summer, while Huntsville and Birmingham agents see steadier volume. Either way, set the monthly premium aside from each closing so a slow month doesn't put coverage at risk.

FAQ

Realtors: questions

What health insurance do realtors get?

Most buy their own. Healthy agents above the subsidy line often choose a private PPO priced on health rather than income.

Can real estate agents deduct health insurance?

Usually, yes. Agents who are independent contractors with a net profit can generally deduct premiums on Form 7206.

Should realtors take the Marketplace subsidy in advance?

Be careful. If your income ends up higher than estimated, excess advance credits must be repaid in full starting with tax year 2026. Healthy agents often avoid that risk with a private PPO.

Does my brokerage have to offer health insurance?

Usually not, because agents are typically independent contractors. A plan you own stays with you if you change brokerages.

Does it cost anything to use Stratton Advisory Group?

No. Carriers pay the broker, and your premium is the same whether you buy directly or through us.

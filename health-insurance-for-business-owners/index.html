Health Insurance for Business Owners and Entrepreneurs in Florida

Owner-only, a few employees, or a growing team: your options change at each stage. We compare individual, private, group, level-funded and ICHRA options across carriers. Free to you.

Key points
  • Owner-only (no employees): compare individual PPO, private underwritten and Marketplace plans.
  • At least one eligible employee who isn't your spouse: small group, level-funded, ICHRA and QSEHRA become options.
  • Self-employed people may be able to deduct health insurance premiums, subject to IRS rules. Confirm with your CPA.
  • The best structure depends on headcount, budget, employee needs and your own family's health.
Ayoub’s take

With owners, I separate two questions: what's right for your own family, and what's right for your team. Trying to answer both with one plan usually ends in a compromise that fits neither.

Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749)

Owning a business changes how you buy health insurance. There's no HR department choosing a plan for you, and the options depend on whether the business has employees, how many, and how you want to fund coverage.

Stage 1: You are the business (owner-only)

Owner-only businesses generally buy individual coverage, not group coverage. In Florida that usually means comparing an individual PPO or EPO, a private medically underwritten plan (often a PPO, subject to health approval) and a Marketplace plan. Marketplace tax credits depend on household income, which can swing year to year for owners, so we look at a realistic income range rather than a single number.

Self-employed people with net profit who aren't eligible for an employer plan may be able to deduct premiums for themselves, a spouse and dependents. S-corporation owners have specific rules for how premiums are reported. Coordinate the details with your CPA; we coordinate with them on plan selection. See self-employed health insurance in Florida.

Stage 2: Your first employees

Once you have at least one eligible employee who isn't your spouse, you can usually consider:

  • Small group plan: one plan (or a few plan choices) for the team, with an employer contribution. Carriers set participation and contribution rules.
  • Level-funded plan: fixed monthly cost with a claims fund; may return a surplus in good years. Usually requires health information from the group.
  • ICHRA: you reimburse employees, tax-free, for individual coverage they choose, up to amounts you set by employee class.
  • QSEHRA: a simpler reimbursement arrangement for employers with fewer than 50 full-time equivalents that don't offer a group plan, with annual limits set by the IRS.

See small business health insurance and our Florida small business guide.

Stage 3: A growing team

As headcount grows, the questions become plan design, contribution strategy, and keeping coverage competitive for hiring. We compare fully insured, level-funded and ICHRA approaches each year at renewal.

Covering your own family well

Many owners want a broader network for their own family than the group plan offers. Depending on the structure, that may mean a higher plan tier within the group, or individual coverage for the family alongside an ICHRA. We map this out with you and your CPA.

We are not tax advisors. Deductions, S-corporation reporting and HRA rules have tax consequences. We explain the options and work alongside your CPA.

Owners who want a PPO for their own family can find one through individual, private or some group designs; compare PPO options.

FAQ

Business owner health insurance questions

Can an owner-only LLC buy a group health plan?

Generally no. Small group coverage requires at least one eligible employee other than the owner and spouse. Owner-only businesses usually compare individual, private and Marketplace plans.

What is an ICHRA?

An Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement lets an employer reimburse employees, tax-free, for individual health insurance they choose, up to amounts the employer sets for each employee class.

Is a level-funded plan right for a small business?

It can be for groups with generally healthy employees who want predictable monthly costs and the chance of a surplus refund. It usually requires group health information and isn't right for every group.

Can I deduct my health insurance as a business owner?

Self-employed people with net profit who aren't eligible for an employer plan may be able to deduct premiums, subject to IRS rules. Ask your CPA how it applies to you.

Do business owners pay for your advice?

No. Whether you buy for yourself or your team, we're paid by the carrier and the premium is unchanged.

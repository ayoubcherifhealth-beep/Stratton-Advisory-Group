Health Insurance for Financial Advisors & Consultants

Health insurance for advisors breaking away from a firm and independent consultants who travel to clients: private PPO plans and national networks.

Reviewed by Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749) · Updated November 17, 2026

Book a Free Consultation Call Us Now

Advisors leaving a wirehouse and consultants going independent lose employer coverage at the same moment their income becomes less predictable. A private PPO with a national network suits healthy earners who travel to clients, and premiums are generally deductible.

Breaking away without a gap

Advisors leaving a wirehouse, bank or large RIA usually plan the book, the custodian and the registration months in advance, and leave health insurance until the last week. The simplest move is to apply for a private PPO while the firm plan is still active, so the new plan starts the day the old one ends. If you'll hire, compare a small group plan with an ICHRA once the practice is running.

Smaller RIAs generally register with their state securities regulator rather than the SEC; the table below lists each state's regulator. Your health plan is separate from registration, but the timing usually lines up.

What matters for financial advisors and consultants

  • Breaking away: plan coverage before your departure date to avoid COBRA.
  • Client travel: a national PPO network keeps care in network on the road.
  • S-corp owners: premiums are handled through payroll before the owner deducts them; ask your CPA.

Between jobs or retiring early?

A gap in coverage is where people overpay most, usually on COBRA. For healthy people a private PPO usually costs less and can start any time. See health insurance between jobs.

The honest caveat: private PPO plans are medically underwritten. They can limit or exclude pre-existing conditions and approval isn't guaranteed. If someone in your household has a serious ongoing condition, a Marketplace plan can be the better choice because it can't turn you down, and we'll tell you if that's you.

Alabama: Birmingham's banks and Huntsville's engineers

Birmingham is Alabama's banking center, home to the headquarters of large regional banks, and many advisors there leave a bank or wirehouse to go independent. Huntsville's engineers and federal contractors make up a large share of clients for advisors in the north.

Birmingham advisors mostly meet clients locally, but many Huntsville and Gulf Coast advisors have clients who retired to Florida or Tennessee. FAQ

Financial Advisors & Consultants: questions

What health insurance should an independent financial advisor get?

For most healthy advisors, a private PPO with a national network, priced on health rather than income. Premiums are generally deductible.

How do consultants get health insurance?

Usually by buying their own. A private PPO with a national network suits consultants who travel to clients.

When should an advisor set up health insurance before going independent?

Before the last day at the firm. Approval for a private plan can take a few weeks, so start early enough to avoid relying on COBRA.

Can my RIA give employees a health benefit without a group plan?

Yes, an ICHRA reimburses employees' individual-market plans up to a set amount.

Does it cost anything to use Stratton Advisory Group?

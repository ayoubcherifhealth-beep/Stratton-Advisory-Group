What happens to your current health insurance when you move to Florida?

It depends on where your coverage comes from. The four most common situations work differently:

You bought an individual plan in your home state

Exchange plans such as NY State of Health, Get Covered New Jersey, Access Health CT or Covered California are sold for a specific state and service area. After a permanent move, the plan's network generally won't cover routine care in Florida, and you will need a Florida plan. Emergencies are covered out of state on ACA-compliant plans, but that is not a substitute for a local network.

You have coverage through an employer

If you keep your job and work remotely from Florida, ask HR whether the plan has a national PPO network or a Florida network. Many large employers offer one. HMO and EPO plans built around another state's hospitals usually won't work for routine care here.

You're leaving a job (COBRA)

COBRA keeps your former employer's exact plan, usually for up to 18 months, at the full premium plus up to 2%. That is useful only if the network reaches your new Florida doctors. Compare it with Florida options before your election deadline. See COBRA alternatives in Florida.

You're retiring before 65

Early retirees moving to Florida usually compare COBRA, an ACA Marketplace plan and a private plan until Medicare eligibility. Income in the first years of retirement can change Marketplace pricing. See early retirement health insurance in Florida.

Do you qualify for a Special Enrollment Period after moving to Florida?

Usually yes, if you had health coverage before the move. CMS guidance for HealthCare.gov states says a permanent move to a new state can qualify you for a Special Enrollment Period, but you generally must have had qualifying coverage for one or more days during the 60 days before your move. The requirement doesn't apply if you are moving from a foreign country or a U.S. territory. Moving only for medical treatment, or staying somewhere on vacation, doesn't qualify.

Not every move qualifies, and HealthCare.gov may ask for documents. If you don't qualify, your choices are a private underwritten plan (if you're healthy), coverage through an employer, or waiting for open enrollment. We check this with you before you apply.

Florida residency: what matters for health insurance

Marketplace plans require you to live in the plan's service area. If you are moving permanently, enroll for the county where you will live. If you will split the year between Florida and another state, choose your plan state carefully, because networks are regional. See our guide for snowbirds and seasonal residents. Questions about legal domicile and taxes belong with your attorney or CPA; we coordinate with them on the health insurance side.

Florida provider networks are regional

Large Florida health systems are concentrated by region, and carriers build different networks in South Florida, Southwest Florida, Tampa Bay, Central Florida and Northeast Florida. A plan that includes your doctors in Naples may not include the system you'd use in Palm Beach. Before recommending anything, we check your specific doctors, hospitals and prescriptions against each plan's directory and formulary.

Moving as a business owner or self-employed professional

Many people relocating to Florida run their own firms or work as consultants. Your options depend on whether you have employees: owner-only businesses usually compare individual, private and Marketplace plans, while businesses with employees can add group, level-funded or ICHRA options. See health insurance for business owners and self-employed health insurance in Florida.

FAQ

Moving to Florida: health insurance questions

Does my health insurance work if I move to Florida?

It depends on the plan. Individual and family plans bought through another state's exchange are tied to that state's service area, so routine care in Florida is usually not covered after a permanent move. Some employer plans with a national PPO network do work in Florida. Check your plan documents or call the number on your card before you move.

Is moving to Florida a qualifying life event?

A permanent move to a new state can qualify you for a 60-day Special Enrollment Period on HealthCare.gov. Under CMS rules, you generally must have had qualifying health coverage for at least one day in the 60 days before the move. That requirement does not apply if you are moving from a foreign country or a U.S. territory. A move only for medical treatment or a vacation does not qualify.

When is open enrollment in Florida?

Florida uses HealthCare.gov. According to CMS, open enrollment for 2027 coverage runs November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15, 2026 for coverage that starts January 1, 2027.

Can I buy private health insurance in Florida outside open enrollment?

Medically underwritten private plans are not limited to open enrollment, so healthy applicants can usually apply at any time of year. The carrier reviews your health history and can decline coverage or exclude pre-existing conditions. ACA Marketplace plans require open enrollment or a Special Enrollment Period.

Should I keep COBRA after moving to Florida?

COBRA continues your former employer's plan, usually for up to 18 months, at the full premium plus up to 2%. It only makes sense after a move if the plan's network includes Florida doctors. If it doesn't, you may pay a high premium for coverage you can't use for routine care. We compare COBRA with your Florida options before your election deadline.

What documents prove Florida residency for health insurance?

For a Marketplace Special Enrollment Period you may be asked for proof of your prior and new address (for example a Florida lease, deed, utility bill or driver license) and proof of prior coverage. HealthCare.gov tells you exactly which documents to upload after you apply.

Can you help me if I still live in New York or New Jersey?

We are licensed in Florida and 30 other states, but not in New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, California, Massachusetts or Pennsylvania. We can explain your Florida options before you move and help you enroll once Florida is your residence. For coverage in your current state, contact a broker licensed there or your state exchange.

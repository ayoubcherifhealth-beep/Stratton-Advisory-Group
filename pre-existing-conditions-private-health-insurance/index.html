Pre-Existing Conditions, Outside the ACA

Pre-existing condition? You may have options outside the ACA.

Many individuals and self-employed professionals earn too much to qualify for ACA subsidies or have fluctuating incomes that make Marketplace coverage less predictable. We explore coverage options beyond the ACA Marketplace, including private PPO plans, to find solutions that align with your budget, health history, and coverage needs.

Key points
  • We compare all your options, including private PPO plans that let you keep seeing your specialists without referrals.
  • Private plans don't use your income to decide eligibility or price. There are no income forms, subsidy reconciliations or repayments at tax time.
  • Private plans review your health history, so a pre-existing condition may be covered, limited or excluded depending on the plan.
  • Group coverage through your own business or a spouse's employer can cover pre-existing conditions without using the marketplace.
  • ACA plans remain the only individual plans that must cover pre-existing conditions, so we compare both before you decide.

Why some people want to avoid the ACA marketplace

The clients who ask us about this usually give one of a few reasons:

  • Income doesn't qualify them for help. Without premium tax credits, marketplace premiums can be high, and the plans they see may have narrow networks.
  • Variable income. Self-employed people and commission earners don't want to estimate next year's income, report changes, or risk repaying tax credits when they file.
  • Network and doctor access. They want a specific doctor or hospital that marketplace plans in their county don't include.
  • Timing. Marketplace plans generally require open enrollment or a qualifying life event, and they need coverage now.

Private plans don't look at your income

Private health insurance is sold directly by insurance companies outside the marketplace. Eligibility and price are based on factors like age, location, tobacco use and, for medically underwritten plans, health history. Your income is not part of the decision. That means no income documents, no reporting changes during the year, and no reconciling tax credits on your return, because private plans don't come with them.

The flip side: private plans are not eligible for marketplace premium tax credits, so if your income would qualify for meaningful help, the marketplace may cost less.

PPO access matters more when you have a condition

If you manage a condition, you probably rely on particular specialists. Many private plans use PPO networks, so you can see in-network specialists without referrals, often use providers across state lines, and may have out-of-network benefits. Before you apply, we check that your doctors and specialists are in the plan's PPO directory, and confirm how the plan treats care related to your condition.

How a pre-existing condition is handled on private plans

Because most private individual plans are medically underwritten, the carrier reviews your health history. Depending on the plan, the condition and how well it's managed, the outcome may be:

Possible outcomeWhat it means
Approved at a lower rateIf the carrier doesn't see you as a higher risk, for example with a minor or well-controlled condition, you may qualify for a lower rate than a guaranteed-issue plan.
Approved at standard ratesSome well-controlled or older conditions may not affect approval or price.
Approved with a limitation or exclusionThe plan covers you, but care related to that condition may be limited or excluded, sometimes for a set period.
DeclinedThe carrier won't offer that plan. Other carriers may decide differently.

Every carrier underwrites differently. Part of our job is knowing which carriers are more likely to accept a given condition, so you don't apply blindly and collect declines.

Important: Not every private plan is ACA-compliant. Some plans sold outside the marketplace, including medically underwritten plans, are not required to follow all ACA rules, so they may limit or exclude pre-existing conditions and may not cover every essential health benefit, such as maternity care. We review each plan's terms with you before you apply, and approval is never guaranteed.

If you need a PPO but have a condition a private plan might exclude, check whether a Marketplace PPO is offered in your county. Our PPO guide walks through both paths.

Other ways to get covered outside the marketplace

  • Group coverage through your own business. Fully insured small group plans can't turn employees down for health conditions. If you own a business with at least one eligible employee besides yourself or a spouse, depending on carrier and state rules, a group plan may be an option. See small business health benefits.
  • A spouse's or partner's employer plan. Employer plans cover pre-existing conditions, and joining is often worth comparing.
  • COBRA. If you're leaving a job, COBRA keeps your current coverage, including your condition, typically for up to 18 months.

When the ACA is still the better choice

If your condition requires ongoing, expensive care, such as cancer treatment, dialysis or a recent major surgery, a private plan may exclude exactly what you need. ACA plans must cover pre-existing conditions and can't charge more for them. We'll tell you plainly if the marketplace is the safer route, even if it isn't your first preference.

FAQ

Pre-Existing Conditions, Outside the ACA: common questions

Can I get private health insurance with a pre-existing condition?

Sometimes. Private plans review your health history, and depending on the condition and the carrier you may be approved, possibly at a lower or standard rate, approved with a limitation, or declined. We help you find carriers more likely to accept your situation.

Do private health plans look at my income?

No. Private plans don't use income to decide eligibility or price. They also don't come with marketplace tax credits, so there's nothing to reconcile when you file taxes.

Will a private plan cover my condition?

It depends on the plan. Some cover the condition fully, some limit or exclude it, sometimes for a set period. We review the exact terms with you before you apply.

What if every private carrier declines me?

Then we look at group coverage through a business you own, a spouse's employer plan, COBRA, or an ACA plan during open enrollment or a special enrollment period.

Is it safe to share my health history?

Yes. We discuss it privately by phone, and we only share it with a carrier when you decide to apply. Please don't put medical details in our online forms.

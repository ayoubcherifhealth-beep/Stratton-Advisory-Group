Health Insurance for Attorneys

Health insurance for associates leaving a firm, new partners and solo practitioners: private PPO plans, the self-employed deduction and small-firm benefits.

Reviewed by Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749) · Updated November 17, 2026

Book a Free Consultation Call Us Now

Leaving a firm, making partner or opening your own practice each change how you get health insurance. Partners and solos are generally self-employed for tax purposes, and healthy attorneys above the subsidy line usually find a private PPO is better value than a full-price Marketplace plan or COBRA.

Associates, partners and solos need different things

An associate leaving a firm needs coverage that starts the day the firm plan ends. A new equity partner is generally treated as self-employed for tax purposes and may be able to deduct premiums. A solo attorney is the employer, with every decision about coverage on their own desk. Bar association offerings exist in some places, but some are limited-benefit plans rather than full comprehensive coverage, so check for an out-of-pocket maximum before relying on one.

For healthy attorneys above the subsidy line, a private PPO with a national network usually beats both COBRA and a full-price Marketplace plan, and it covers you when cases or clients take you out of state.

What matters for attorneys

  • Leaving a firm: line up a private PPO instead of paying full price for COBRA.
  • Partners: equity partners are generally treated as self-employed; premiums may be deductible.
  • Bar association plans: confirm any association offering is full comprehensive coverage, not a limited-benefit plan.

Between jobs or retiring early?

A gap in coverage is where people overpay most, usually on COBRA. For healthy people a private PPO usually costs less and can start any time. See health insurance between jobs.

The honest caveat: private PPO plans are medically underwritten. They can limit or exclude pre-existing conditions and approval isn't guaranteed. If someone in your household has a serious ongoing condition, a Marketplace plan can be the better choice because it can't turn you down, and we'll tell you if that's you.

Alabama: Birmingham's firms and the capital in Montgomery

Birmingham has the state's largest law firms and corporate legal departments, Montgomery has the state courts and agencies, and Huntsville's federal and defense contractors keep government-contracts lawyers busy. Many Alabama attorneys practice solo or in small firms outside the big cities.

Alabama litigators often drive between Birmingham, Montgomery and county courthouses across the state, and Huntsville government-contracts lawyers fly to Washington. A statewide network is the minimum; a national PPO matters if you try cases or meet clients out of state. Families in Shelby County usually want UAB and Grandview-area doctors on the same plan.

FAQ

Attorneys: questions

What is the best health insurance for a solo attorney?

For most healthy solos, a private PPO with a national network. Premiums are generally deductible on Form 7206 when the practice has a net profit.

Is a bar association health plan good coverage?

Some are; some are limited-benefit plans. Confirm it is full comprehensive coverage with an out-of-pocket maximum before relying on it.

What should I do about health insurance when I leave my firm?

Apply for a private PPO before your last day so it starts when the firm plan ends. COBRA or state continuation is available, but at the full premium.

Are equity partners self-employed for health insurance?

Generally yes for tax purposes, which means premiums may be deductible. Your accountant will confirm how your partnership handles it.

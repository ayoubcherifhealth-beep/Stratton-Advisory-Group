Health Insurance for Travel Nurses in Florida

Agency plans end when the contract ends, and deductibles reset with every new agency. Your own PPO with a national network stays with you between assignments and can keep you in network wherever you take a contract, if the network is national.

Reviewed by Ayoub Cherif, licensed health insurance broker (NPN 22089749) · Updated November 15, 2026

Book a Free Consultation Call Us Now
Key points
  • Agency health plans usually end with the contract or shortly after, and some agencies only bridge short gaps.
  • Changing agencies means a new plan, a new network and a new deductible.
  • Your own PPO with a national network stays the same from assignment to assignment, with in-network care across the U.S.
  • If you work as an independent 1099 nurse with a net profit, your premiums are usually deductible.

Many travel nurses carry their own health insurance instead of relying on each agency's plan. A private PPO with a national network keeps the same doctors, the same deductible progress and the same coverage whether your next contract is in Gainesville, Dallas or Denver, and it doesn't stop when an assignment ends.

The problem with agency coverage

Agency benefits are convenient on day one, but they are tied to the contract:

  • Gaps between assignments. Coverage often ends when the contract ends. Some agencies extend it for a short break if your next contract is already signed with them; longer breaks usually aren't covered.
  • A new deductible with every agency. Switching agencies means starting your deductible and out-of-pocket maximum over again.
  • New networks. Each agency picks its own carrier and network, so a doctor you like may be in network one quarter and out the next.

If you lose agency coverage, that loss generally opens a Special Enrollment Period for a Marketplace plan, and the agency may offer COBRA at the full premium. Both are ways to patch a gap. Your own plan removes the gap.

Why a national PPO is the best fit for travelers

Travel nurses need in-network care in more than one state. An HMO or EPO only pays for non-emergency care inside its network, and many Florida Marketplace plans use regional networks. A PPO with a national network lets you see in-network doctors and specialists without referrals wherever you take an assignment, and still keep your providers back home in Florida.

Many travelers keep their own plan and decline the agency's. Some agencies pay more when you waive their benefits, so ask your recruiter whether that applies to your contract before you compare costs.

Why the ACA is usually not the best route for travel nurses

  • Income is hard to estimate. Marketplace tax credits depend on your yearly income, which changes with contracts and breaks. Starting with tax year 2026, excess advance credits must be repaid in full.
  • No subsidy above the cliff. For 2027, households above 400% of the federal poverty level pay full price, and a year of strong contracts can put you there.
  • Regional networks. Many Florida Marketplace plans are HMO or EPO, which doesn't fit a job that moves.
  • Enrollment windows. Outside Open Enrollment, you need a qualifying event. Many private plans accept applications year-round.

See the full comparison in private health insurance vs. ACA plans in Florida.

Private plans are not guaranteed. Private, medically underwritten plans ask health questions, can limit or exclude pre-existing conditions and approval is up to the carrier. If you have a serious ongoing condition, we compare the Marketplace with you too, because it accepts everyone during Open Enrollment.

W-2 traveler or independent 1099 nurse?

Most travel nurses are W-2 employees of their agency, so the self-employed premium deduction doesn't apply to those wages. Nurses who work independently on 1099 contracts, through platforms or direct with facilities, can generally deduct premiums against a net profit, calculated on IRS Form 7206, for months they weren't eligible for an employer-subsidized plan. See health insurance for 1099 contractors.

Two examples

These are illustrations of common situations, not client stories.

Example 1. An ICU travel nurse based in Tampa takes three 13-week contracts a year with two different agencies and a month off in summer. She qualifies for a private PPO with a national network, waives agency coverage and keeps one plan, one deductible and her Tampa doctors all year.

Example 2. A per diem nurse in Orlando works independent 1099 shifts at several facilities. He compares a full-price Marketplace EPO with a private PPO, chooses the PPO and deducts the premium against his net self-employment income.

We are not tax advisors. Whether and how much you can deduct depends on your net profit and other coverage you could get. We handle the coverage side and work alongside your CPA.

More for travelers: health insurance between travel nursing assignments covers what to do when a contract ends.

Coverage gaps happen. Between jobs, during a new employer's waiting period or after an early retirement, a private PPO is often the better bridge for healthy people than full-price COBRA. See health insurance between jobs and early retirement health insurance.

Fort Lauderdale: Fort Lauderdale hospitals and your compact license

Broward Health, Memorial Healthcare System and Holy Cross keep demand for travelers high in Broward County, and Cleveland Clinic Florida in Weston adds specialty contracts. Florida is a compact state, so Florida-based nurses can work in most other states on one license.

Confirm Broward Health, Memorial, Holy Cross and Cleveland Clinic Florida for time at home, then the out-of-state systems where you'll take summer contracts.

Jacksonville: Jacksonville hospitals and your compact license

Mayo Clinic in Florida, Baptist Health and UF Health Jacksonville bring travel nurses to Jacksonville, and Southeast Georgia is a short drive north. Florida and Georgia are both compact states.

Confirm Mayo, Baptist and UF Health Jacksonville, then Georgia providers if you take Brunswick or Savannah contracts.

Miami: Miami hospitals and your compact license

Jackson Health System, Baptist Health South Florida and UHealth employ thousands of nurses, and Miami's hurricane season and winter population swell create strong demand for travelers.

Confirm Jackson, Baptist Health South Florida and UHealth, then the states where you'll work outside the winter season.

Naples: Naples hospitals and your compact license

NCH and Physicians Regional see their busiest months in winter, when Naples' seasonal population arrives, so many travelers take winter contracts in Naples.

Confirm NCH and Physicians Regional for winter contracts, then wherever you'll spend summer, often near family up north.

Orlando: Orlando hospitals and your compact license

AdventHealth and Orlando Health are among Florida's largest employers of nurses, and the Lake Nona medical city has added new hospitals.

Confirm AdventHealth and Orlando Health at home. If you take contracts out of state, a national network keeps you covered there.

Tampa: Tampa hospitals and your compact license

Tampa General, BayCare and AdventHealth's Tampa Bay hospitals bring travelers to the region, and Moffitt Cancer Center adds oncology contracts.

Confirm Tampa General, BayCare and Moffitt at home, then out-of-state systems for your other contracts.

FAQ

Travel nurse health insurance questions

Do travel nurses get health insurance?

Most travel nursing agencies offer health insurance during an assignment, but it's usually tied to the contract and can end when the contract ends. Many travelers buy their own plan so coverage doesn't stop between assignments.

What is the best health insurance for travel nurses?

For most travelers, a PPO with a national network. It covers in-network doctors and specialists in every state you work, keeps one deductible all year and doesn't end when a contract ends.

What happens to my insurance between travel nursing contracts?

Agency coverage often ends with the contract or shortly after. Losing it generally opens a Special Enrollment Period, and the agency may offer COBRA at full price. Your own year-round plan avoids the gap.

Should I waive my agency's health insurance?

It can make sense if you have your own plan. Some agencies pay more when you waive benefits, so compare the pay difference with the cost of your own PPO before you decide.

Can travel nurses deduct health insurance?

Independent 1099 nurses with a net profit can usually deduct premiums on IRS Form 7206. W-2 travelers generally can't take the self-employed deduction on agency wages. Ask your tax professional.

Can I get private health insurance with a health condition?

Private plans ask health questions and can limit or exclude pre-existing conditions, so approval isn't guaranteed. If you have a serious ongoing condition, we compare Marketplace plans with you too.
